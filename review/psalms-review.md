# Psalms review

All 150 psalms, 2,461 WEB verses, and publisher headings were read. Psalm 119's alphabet labels remain outside the verse text. Each psalm has an original explanation and a specific question or later Church reading.

The guide uses Isaiah's reader, Map and Read controls, chapter selector, settings, and profiles. Notes name the speaker's claims and retain difficult passages, including Psalms 58, 69, 88, 109, 137, and 149.

## Sources

- Luther Seminary: complete main Summary and Background pages. The Summary provides general poetry and worship context. It is not presented as commentary on every verse.
- Luther Seminary: complete main Summary and Analysis sections for Psalms 22, 23, 44, 73, 88–89, and 137. Linked passages were not separately reviewed.
- Daniel McClellan: the author's uploaded copy of the 2018 Journal of Biblical Literature article. Review covers the abstract, opening discussion, and sections IV–V on pages 847–851. The Psalm 82 note concerns his reading of a divine assembly. The printed DOI is recorded.
- Wikipedia: Psalms revision 1370187881. Review covers the opening overview, headings section, bibliography, revision footer, and CC BY-SA 4.0 license. Alter's cited book was checked against Norton's publisher record. That record verifies publication, not unread commentary.
- Come, Follow Me 2026 lessons 34–36: complete main and children’s sections. Coverage follows assigned or explicitly discussed psalms. Psalm 134 is explicitly discussed in lesson 36.
- Seminary student lessons 113 and 115: complete main lessons and questions. Linked videos were not reviewed. Specific notes distinguish later Church readings from a poem’s speaker.

## People and maps

All 39 profiles have reader links. Eight new portraits vary faces, expressions, poses, framing, garments, light, and settings. Thirty-one portraits reuse reviewed records for already depicted people.

Heading names receive short context in selected introductions. Heman and Ethan the Ezrahites have separate profiles. Cush and Abimelech in these headings do not share profiles with Genesis namesakes. Poetic Rahab does not link to the woman in Joshua. Jacob does not replace every reference to Israel.

Fifteen map places provide reference areas for explicit names, Zion, and Egypt's poetic name in Psalm 87. The songs receive no invented event years or travel routes. Sinai retains its uncertain traditional location.

## Checks

The Bible and writing checks cover verse sequence, perspective source coverage, saved portraits, short sentences, and source records. Focused checks cover profile access, namesakes, alphabet labels, Psalm 82's source, and the absence of invented routes. Browser review checks the reader, perspective controls, maps, and portraits.
